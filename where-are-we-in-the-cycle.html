A description, not a forecast

The score tells you how stretched the prices behind cyclical earnings are compared with their own history. It does not tell you when they will turn. A high reading can last for months, and a low one can get lower.

Use it as a starting point, then look at the signal that matters for the stocks you follow. Every signal page links to the companies most tied to it.

How the score is calculated

Every signal has a BUY level, a near-sell level and a SELL level, set from its own history (see how it works). Its latest value puts it in one of four zones, and each zone is worth a fixed number of points:

ZonePoints
BUY15
NEUTRAL50
NEAR SELL72
SELL90
  • The score is the average of those points across all tracked signals, rounded to a whole number. So in practice it runs from 15 (every signal in BUY) to 90 (every signal in SELL).
  • Every signal counts the same. Brent crude and salmon have equal weight. The score is not weighted by market size or by how many stocks follow each signal.
  • Two series are left out: the Brent–WTI spread, because it is calculated from two other signals, and the US yield curve, which is a recession indicator rather than a price cycle.
  • Zones move in steps. A signal moving within its zone doesn’t change the score. It only changes when a signal crosses a level.
  • Hand-updated signals count with their last value. If one hasn’t been updated for a while, the score reflects that old value.

Questions

What is the Signycle cycle score?

It is one number that sums up where Signycle’s tracked commodity, freight and macro signals sit against their own historical BUY and SELL levels. Each signal’s zone is turned into points (BUY 15, neutral 50, near sell 72, SELL 90) and the score is the average. A low score means most signals are historically cheap; a high score means most are historically expensive.

Does a high score mean I should sell cyclical stocks?

No. The score describes where prices are against their history; it does not say when they will turn, and prices can stay above their SELL levels for long stretches. BUY and SELL levels are reference points drawn from the past, not forecasts. Nothing on Signycle is financial advice.

Is this the same as the economic cycle?

No. The score measures the prices that drive cyclical companies’ earnings, such as oil, metals, freight rates and interest rates. It does not measure GDP, jobs or company profits directly. For recession risk, see the recession tracker.

How often is the score updated?

It is recalculated in your browser every time the page loads signal data, and again every couple of minutes while the page is open. Signals priced live from Yahoo Finance, the ECB and FRED refresh about every 15 minutes. The others are updated by hand from published sources and count with their last entered value.

Why might part of the reading be out of date?

Hand-updated signals, such as freight indices and PMI, only change when a new value is entered. Each one shows the date it was last updated, and the score card above says how many are more than 45 days old. An old value still counts toward the score, so a stale signal can hold the score in place.
