🇬🇧 London LSE Mining Sector

London is the world's premier mining stock exchange. Rio Tinto, BHP, Glencore, Anglo American and Antofagasta together account for roughly 15% of the FTSE 100 — making LSE uniquely sensitive to commodity supercycles.

Why London Dominates Global Mining

London's position as the global mining capital stems from its role as the home of the London Metal Exchange (LME) — the world's primary copper, aluminium and zinc futures market. Major miners choose a London listing to access the deepest pool of commodity-specialist institutional capital.

Rio Tinto vs BHP — Iron Ore Focus

Both Rio Tinto and BHP derive approximately 50-60% of EBITDA from iron ore, making them primarily iron ore plays despite their diversification. Iron ore at $/t is in neutral territory — not yet cheap enough to signal a strong buy, but not at the elevated $150-160/t levels that historically mark a sell.

Glencore — The Trader

Glencore is unique among LSE miners — it combines mining assets with one of the world's largest commodity trading operations. This means Glencore benefits from volatility as well as from high prices, giving it a different risk profile to pure miners like Rio Tinto. Copper at $/t with Glencore in sell zone suggests caution on the mining assets, but the trading division may offset weakness.
