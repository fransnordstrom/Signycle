What Drives Copper Prices?

China (58% of demand) — The single biggest driver. Chinese industrial production, infrastructure investment and manufacturing PMI directly correlate with copper demand. When China's PMI is above 52, copper typically rises. When it falls below 49, copper corrects.

Energy transition — Electric vehicles use 4x more copper than internal combustion engines. Solar panels, wind turbines and grid infrastructure all require significant copper. This structural demand is creating a long-term supply deficit that analysts estimate will persist through 2030+.

Mine supply — The world's largest copper mines (Escondida, Grasberg, Kamoa-Kakula) frequently experience disruptions. Mine grades are declining globally, requiring more ore to be processed for the same copper output. This structural supply tightness supports elevated prices.

US tariff uncertainty — In 2025-2026, uncertainty around US copper import tariffs caused a significant price distortion between COMEX (US) and LME (global) prices. This created a $2,000+/t premium on COMEX and drove speculative inventory builds in the US.

FAQ — Copper Stocks

Is now a good time to buy copper stocks?
Based on the LME copper cycle signal, no — at least not for new positions. Copper at /t is 41% above the Signycle SELL threshold of $9,000/t. Historically, buying copper stocks when copper is above $9,000 has delivered poor risk-adjusted returns. The best entry points have been at or below $5,000/t. The current cycle is late — patience is required for the next great entry.
Which copper stock has the most pure copper exposure?
Antofagasta (ANTO, London) is the purest large-cap copper play — approximately 80% of revenue from copper. Freeport-McMoRan (FCX, NYSE) is the world's largest listed copper producer with similarly high copper exposure. Southern Copper (SCCO) and First Quantum Minerals (FM) also have high copper purity. Glencore, Rio Tinto and Anglo American are more diversified with significant iron ore, coal and other metal exposure.
What is the difference between COMEX and LME copper prices?
LME (London Metal Exchange) sets the global benchmark copper price, quoted in USD per metric tonne. COMEX (Chicago Mercantile Exchange) quotes in USD per pound. In 2025-2026, US tariff uncertainty caused an unusual premium on COMEX vs LME of up to $2,000/mt as traders stockpiled copper in US warehouses. Signycle tracks the LME price as the global benchmark.
